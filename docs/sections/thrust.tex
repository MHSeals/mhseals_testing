\section{Speed curves and per-direction coupling}
\label{sec:thrust}

The affine model of Section~\ref{sec:char} has one gain per axis. A boat with
quadratic drag does not have one: the secant gain fitted at one effort is wrong
by tens of percent at another. This section replaces it with a curve per axis
and sign, and replaces the decoupling $C = G^{-1}D$ of Section~\ref{sec:mixer}
with one column per axis and sign.

\subsection{Speed curves}

A T200's thrust grows with $e|e|$, not with $e$. In normalized wrench units
$w = e|e|$, a hold on one axis at effort $e$ settles where thrust meets drag:
\[
  e|e| = d_1 v + d_2 v|v| ,\qquad d_1, d_2 \ge 0 .
\]
Each of the six directions (\code{surge+} \dots\ \code{yaw-}) has its own pair
$(d_1, d_2)$, fitted by least squares on the hold means at two or more effort
levels. A negative term (noise, or a single level) is dropped and the other is
refitted, so every curve rises with speed and can be inverted: the controller
asks for $v^\star$ and drives $e = \operatorname{sign}(w)\sqrt{|w|}$ with
$w = d_1 v^\star + d_2 v^\star|v^\star|$.

Speeds are through the water: the world drift of Section~\ref{sec:char} is
turned into the body at each sample's heading and removed before the fit, and
only samples that command a single axis enter, so cross coupling never bends a
curve. Because the coupling columns below keep $c_{jj} = 1$, the main speed is
unchanged by $C$ to first order, and the curves stay valid after it changes.

\subsection{One column per axis and sign}

The driver applies
\[
  \bm e' = C^{+}\max(\bm e, 0) + C^{-}\min(\bm e, 0),\qquad c^{\pm}_{jj} = 1,
\]
before the mixer: column $j$ of $C^{+}$ serves axis $j$ driven forward, column
$j$ of $C^{-}$ the same axis driven backward. With $C^{+} = C^{-}$ this is the
single matrix of Section~\ref{sec:mixer}.

\paragraph{Why a column does not depend on the effort.} Hold axis $j$ with no
cross velocity. Every Coriolis and added-mass term is a product of two velocity
components, so all vanish, and the steady balance is
$W(\bm e) + H(V\hat{\bm e}_j) = D_j(V)\,\hat{\bm e}_j$, with $H$ the hull's cross
terms at main speed $V$. If (i) the cross drag at zero cross velocity is
diagonal, (ii) each thruster curve is homogeneous per sign,
$f(\lambda t) = \lambda^n f(t)$, and (iii) $H_x(V)/D_j(V)$ does not depend on
$V$, then a column that nulls the cross terms at one amplitude nulls them at
every amplitude. Condition (ii) fails inside the T200 deadband (about $0.06$),
and (iii) fails for linear-plus-quadratic drag, so a characterization checks
the column again at a lower amplitude.

\paragraph{Why the sign matters.} A single matrix holds only the odd part of the
coupling. The 2026-10-18 field holds turned the boat when it moved forward or
left and hardly at all when it moved back or right: a one-sided coupling, whose
even part $(C^{+} - C^{-})/2$ is as large as its odd part. Two columns per axis
represent odd, even and one-sided terms alike.

\paragraph{Local slope, not secant.} With $\varphi(t) = t|t|$, reverse thrust
ratio $\rho$ and the boat's mixer $M$, the cross wrench produced by a small
change of the cross efforts, while the main effort is $s a$, is
\[
  J_A(j,s,a) = a\left[(1+\rho)\,I_2 - s\,(1-\rho)\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}\right],
\]
well conditioned ($\kappa = 1.28$ at $\rho = 0.78$). The secant gain
$f(a)/a$ is $J_A/n$ with $n = 2$, so $C = G^{-1}D$ built from secant gains
corrects twice as much as it should; simulated boats came out worse than with no
correction in most cases. Characterize therefore no longer offers to install
it. The columns are to be computed from the local slope at the measured state
and refined by Broyden updates, each accepted only when the mismatch exceeds
three standard deviations.

\paragraph{What the mixer guarantees.} Every column of the boat's $M$ sums to
zero. A uniform reverse weakness then produces no cross wrench and equal
forward and reverse main gains; a single thruster with a weak reverse leaks only
in the three directions where it runs reversed, in proportion to its row of $M$,
which the pattern of $C^{\pm}$ reveals.

\paragraph{Trust (planned).} A direction's trust is the probability that every cross
response lies inside its tolerance (yaw $0.015$ rad/s per m/s, crab $0.05$,
rotation centre $0.10$ m for the yaw rows) given its noise. The noise is the
spread of 8\,s \emph{window means} pooled over neutral windows, not the sample
standard error, which the 5\,Hz GPS sample-and-hold makes about $3\times$ too
optimistic. A direction below $80\%$ is rerun.
