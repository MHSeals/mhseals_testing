\section{The wave floor and the sea}
\label{sec:cond-floor}

The pure estimator returns the SD of an 8\,s neutral window mean, ordered
(yaw, surge, sway), once a current drift pool exists. Per-channel spectral levels
use all usable windows, while residuals use the current pool:
\[
 \sigma_{\mathrm{wave},k}^2=\max\left(\frac{S_{0,k}}{\Tw},
             \frac{\sum_{w\in\mathrm{pool}} e_{w,k}^2}{W-1}\right).
\]
Yaw residuals subtract the pool's mean yaw rate. Surge and sway residuals are
$R(\psi_w)^\top(\bm m_w-\dhat)$; they are not separately demeaned after rotation.
The mean floor supplies readiness ceilings.

\paragraph{Saved characterization noise.} The characterization record's
\code{wave\_floor} is a per-sample neutral SD. It skips the configured
transient/coast interval or the last driven axis's $t_{95}$, whichever is larger,
and demeans each retained neutral window before pooling its residuals:
\[
 \sigma_{\mathrm{saved},k}^2=\frac{\sum_w\sum_{i\in w}(x_{i,k}-\bar x_{w,k})^2}{N-1}.
\]
This is distinct from the window-mean floor above.

\subsection{Why the floor is measured, not predicted from the waves}

It is tempting to predict the floor from the wave height. It does not work, and
the reason is instructive. Waves on a pond are narrow-band: a period $T_p$ of 1 to
4\,s. The mean of a sinusoid $A\sin(\omega t + \varphi)$ over a window of length
$T$ is
\[
  \frac1T\int_0^T A\sin(\omega t+\varphi)\,dt = \frac{A}{\omega T}\big(\cos\varphi - \cos(\omega T + \varphi)\big),
\]
and since the bracket is at most 2 in size,
\[
  |\text{mean}| \le \frac{2A}{\omega T} = \frac{A\,T_p}{\pi T}.
\]
An 8\,s window removes at least 88\,\% of a 3\,s swell's amplitude, more for shorter
periods, and the bound falls as $1/T$ (Figure~\ref{fig:cond-narrowband}). So the
swell the eye sees contributes little to the floor. What remains is the slow part:
gusts, weathervaning, slow yaw. Those do not show in a wave-height number, so the
floor is always measured from window means.

\subsection{Sea: shown, never gating}

The service's sea display uses the standard deviation of supplied neutral
yaw-rate samples. The pure \code{sea\_state} function instead accepts native IMU
samples over the last 30\,s, subtracts least-squares linear trends, and reports
RMS yaw rate, RMS combined roll/pitch rate, and RMS vertical specific-force
residual. It requires at least four finite samples and feeds no gate.

\subsection{The gyro bias}

For reliable yaw and at least three finite samples spanning 20\,s, the pure
estimator unwraps compass heading and fits its slope using all samples:
\[
 x_i=t_i-\bar t,\qquad
 b_r=\bar r+\frac{\sum_i x_i(\psi_{c,i}-\bar\psi_c)}{\sum_i x_i^2},\qquad
 \sed(b_r)^2=\frac{\sum_i(\psi_{c,i}-\bar\psi_c-\hat\beta_c x_i)^2}
 {(n-2)\sum_i x_i^2}.
\]
The plus sign cancels a true turn because FLU gyro yaw is counter-clockwise
and compass heading is clockwise. The service does not supply this bias error
to its ceiling calculation.
