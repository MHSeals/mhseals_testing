\section{Navigation: MPPI, the costmap, and how runs are measured}
\label{sec:nav}

\subsection{The MPPI controller}

Nav2 follows its planned path with MPPI (model predictive path integral). Every
control cycle it samples $K$ sequences of commands $(v_x, v_y, \omega)$ over a
horizon of $N$ steps of $\Delta t$, rolls each through a kinematic model of the
boat (Omni: the X thrusters can sway), scores each rollout with a sum of critics
$S_k$ (distance from the path, heading along it, nearness to obstacles, progress to
the goal, turning), and blends them with weights
\[
  w_k = \frac{e^{-(S_k - S_{\min})/\lambda}}{\sum_j e^{-(S_j - S_{\min})/\lambda}},
  \qquad \bm u = \sum_k w_k\,\bm u_k .
\]
The configured Omni controller uses $K=600$, $N=48$, $\Delta t=0.1$\,s
and $\lambda=0.3$, with one iteration and $\gamma=0.015$. Command perturbation
standard deviations are $(0.10,0.07,0.15)$ in surge, sway and yaw rate.
A rotation shim wraps MPPI, with heading threshold $1.8$\,rad and rotation
speed $0.3$\,rad/s; it also turns to the goal heading.

\paragraph{Timing and path horizon.} The configuration derives the model period
from the controller frequency and preserves the configured prune-distance floor:
\[
 \Delta t=\operatorname{round}(1/f,6),\qquad
 D=\max\!\left(D_0,\operatorname{round}(1.2v_{x,\max}N\Delta t,2)\right).
\]
At $f=10$\,Hz the horizon is $4.8$\,s and $D_0=3$\,m. Default velocity
limits are $v_x\in[-0.1,0.4]$\,m/s, $|v_y|\le0.15$\,m/s and
$|\omega|\le0.3$\,rad/s. A characterization overlay can replace the limits.

\subsection{The costmap over open water}

The perception costmap is a rolling $24\times24$\,m grid in \code{odom},
with $0.15$\,m cells and $5$\,Hz updates. Its LiDAR spatio-temporal voxel
layer has a $3$\,s decay. A second layer consumes the object tracker's
\code{/perception/track\_grid}, combining costs by maximum. Both Nav2 maps
consume \code{/perception/costmap} with graded costs preserved, then inflate it.

Static and unknown tracks produce lethal discs. The static disc uses the last
seen position and covariance; its class radius $R$ is expanded by
\[
 R_{\rm disc}=R+\min\!\left(2\sqrt{\max(\lambda_{\max}(P_{xy}),0)},\ 0.1\,\mathrm m\right).
\]
A moving track's hull disc is lethal, its current uncertainty ring costs $99$,
and future discs follow constant velocity in $0.25$\,s steps over a $3$\,s
horizon. At future offset $t>0$, the integer occupancy cost is
\[
 C(t)=\min\!\left(99,\max\!\left(40,\left\lfloor100(1-t/3)\right\rfloor\right)\right).
\]
Prediction includes measurement latency; the remaining horizon shrinks by that
latency. Each predicted disc uses the propagated position covariance and the
same $0.1$\,m expansion cap.

\paragraph{Inflation and the footprint.} The hull is a $1.063\times0.895$\,m
rectangle, padded by
$0.1$\,m. Its padded corner radius is
$r_c=\sqrt{0.6315^2+0.5475^2}\approx0.84$\,m. MPPI's CostCritic enables
footprint checking and samples every second trajectory point.

The Nav2 inflation layer uses radius $1.5$\,m and cost scaling factor $3$.
Beyond the padded inscribed radius $r_i\approx0.55$\,m, its exponential cost
is $252e^{-3(d-r_i)}$ within the inflation radius. The midpoint of a
$1.83$\,m gate is about $0.91$\,m from either buoy, giving cost about $84$.

\paragraph{The planner.} SmacPlanner2D supplies the global path through the
costmap, accounting for cell costs as well as distance.

\subsection{Measuring a run}

\paragraph{Cross-track error.} The route is a polyline through the waypoints,
starting where the boat was when the goal was sent. For a segment from $\bm a$
to $\bm b$ and a position $\bm p$, project onto the segment and clamp to its ends:
\[
  \lambda = \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\frac{(\bm p-\bm a)\cdot(\bm b-\bm a)}{\|\bm b-\bm a\|^2},\,0,\,1\right),\qquad
  d = \left\|\bm p - (\bm a + \lambda(\bm b-\bm a))\right\|.
\]
For a zero-length segment take $\lambda=0$; a singleton route uses distance
to its only point. Fewer than two track samples are INCONCLUSIVE.
The cross-track error of $\bm p$ is the smallest $d$ over all segments. Clamping
matters at corners: without it the distance would be to the infinite line, which
can be small far beyond the end of a leg. The procedure reports the mean, RMS and
maximum, the closest approach to each waypoint, and the travelled path length
against the route length. The reported final error is the closest-ever approach
to the last waypoint $\bm w_{\rm last}$:
\[e_{\rm final}=\min_k\|\bm p_k-\bm w_{\rm last}\|.\]

\paragraph{Station keeping.} With samples $\bm p_k$ at times $t_k$ around a
centre $\bm c$, the radii $\rho_k = \|\bm p_k - \bm c\|$ give the RMS and maximum
radius, and the fraction of time inside the tolerance (each sample weighted by its
following interval; the last sample has zero weight). Fewer than three points
or a nonpositive time span are INCONCLUSIVE. For tolerance $\epsilon$,
\[
 F_{\rm inside}=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{m-2}(t_{k+1}-t_k)\,\mathbf1_{\rho_k\le\epsilon}}{t_{m-1}-t_0},
 \qquad \text{HELD}\iff\max_k\rho_k\le\epsilon.
\]
Otherwise the verdict is LEFT TOLERANCE. A slow steady push that the controller
does not cancel can stay inside
the tolerance for a long time, so the drift is measured directly as the
least-squares slope of each coordinate:
\[
  (\alpha_x, v_x) = \argmin_{\alpha,v} \sum_k \left(x_k - \alpha - v\,(t_k - t_0)\right)^2,
\]
and likewise for $y$. The drift speed is $\sqrt{v_x^2+v_y^2}$ and its heading
$\operatorname{atan2}(v_y, v_x)$.
