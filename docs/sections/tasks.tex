\section{Task geometry}
\label{sec:tasks}

\paragraph{Gates.} Marker pairs are sorted by distance and accepted greedily,
using each marker once, with $w_{\min}\le\|\bm g-\bm r\|\le w_{\max}$
(defaults $1$ and $8$\,m). Same-colour pairing also uses each marker once.
The gate midpoint and port-red normal are
\[
 \bm m=\tfrac12(\bm r+\bm g),\qquad
 \phi=\operatorname{atan2}(g_y-r_y,g_x-r_x)+\tfrac\pi2.
\]
For starboard-red add $\pi$; for \code{any}, add $\pi$ only if
$\cos(\phi-\operatorname{atan2}(m_y-p_y,m_x-p_x))<0$, facing away from the boat.
The channel and follow-path configurations use red to port. Gates are returned
in increasing distance from the origin.

\paragraph{Search.} The channel searches for both marker colours before driving
the gates. GateRun explores ahead when the next gate is not visible, bounded
by its configured search distance and the stage deadline.

\paragraph{Circling.} To circle an object at $\bm o$ with radius $R$ in $n$ steps,
start on the side facing the boat, $\beta = \operatorname{atan2}(p_y - o_y, p_x - o_x)$,
and place waypoints at angles $\beta + s\,2\pi k/n$ for $k = 1\ldots n$, with
$s=+1$ counter-clockwise and $-1$ clockwise. Each waypoint faces along the
circle, angle $+\,s\,\pi/2$, so Nav2 drives around the object rather than at it.

\paragraph{Relative routes.} A route given as (forward, left) offsets
$(f, l)$ from a pose $(x, y, \psi)$ maps to the world by the rotation
$\left(x + f\cos\psi - l\sin\psi,\ y + f\sin\psi + l\cos\psi\right)$.
Each pose faces along the leg from its preceding point.
\paragraph{Passing a gate.} A gate at $\bm m$ with heading $\phi$ is driven
through two poses, $\bm m - 1.5\,\bm f$ and $\bm m + 1.1\,\bm f$ with
$\bm f = (\cos\phi, \sin\phi)$: the second is half a hull ($0.53$\,m) and a margin
past the line, so the whole boat crosses it but the goal does not land on what
lies beyond. GateRun records a near-side approach before accepting a crossing. Write the
current gate-frame coordinates as $a=(\bm p-\bm m)\cdot\bm f$ and
$b=-(p_x-m_x)\sin\phi+(p_y-m_y)\cos\phi$, and the previous coordinates
as $(a_0,b_0)$. The crossing predicate is
\[
 a\ge0.6\quad\land\quad\left[|b|\le2.5\quad\lor\quad
 \left(a_0<0\ \land\ \left|b_0+(b-b_0)\frac{-a_0}{a-a_0}\right|\le2.5\right)\right].
\]
The second branch uses the interpolated intersection when the current sample
is laterally outside. The next gate is the nearest uncleared one ahead along
the last cleared gate's heading (or the boat heading if none is supplied),
within $25$\,m and more than $2$\,m from every cleared gate. The first planned
gate is selected by proximity within the configured first-gate radius.

\paragraph{Docking bays.} Banners stand along the dock's spine. With another banner more than $0.3$\,m away, the spine's direction is the principal axis of their positions,
$\theta = \tfrac12\operatorname{atan2}(2s_{xy}, s_{xx} - s_{yy})$ from their
covariances, and the bays open along its normal on the boat's side. With no such neighbour,
the opening faces the boat. A bay is
taken as empty when no vessel is tracked within $1$\,m of its centre. The boat
berths $1.2$\,m out from the banner: the bow clears the spine's costmap margin and
the configured bay depth is $1.5$\,m.

\paragraph{Counting.} Positions are retained sequentially only if at least $1.5$\,m
from every retained position. This deduplicates nearby reports without merging
transitive chains of positions.

\paragraph{What Nav2 takes literally.} Nav2 judges a route done by the distance to
its last pose, and drops waypoints within a metre of the boat. A full circle, or
a turn-in-place sweep back to the starting heading, ends where the boat already
is and would finish at once. So circles are sent as two halves and sweeps as two
single turns, each ending away from where it began.
