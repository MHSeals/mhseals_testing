\section{Task geometry}
\label{sec:tasks}

\paragraph{Gates.} Red and green markers are paired greedily by distance: the
closest pair first, each marker used once, pairs wider than the maximum gate width
ignored. The gate is the midpoint $\bm m = \tfrac12(\bm r + \bm g)$. Its heading
is normal to the line $\bm r\to\bm g$; of the two normals, the one pointing away
from the boat is kept:
\[
  \phi = \operatorname{atan2}(g_y - r_y, g_x - r_x) + \tfrac\pi2,\qquad
  \text{if } \cos(\phi - \operatorname{atan2}(m_y - p_y, m_x - p_x)) < 0:\ \phi \leftarrow \phi + \pi,
\]
so the boat passes straight through instead of along the line of markers.

\paragraph{Waiting.} A task that waits to see something (the first gate of a
channel) holds where it began waiting. Under wind and current an idle boat does
not stay put: at the $0.5$\,m/s drift of an $8$\,m/s wind over a $0.15$\,m/s
current it is $15$\,m off in
half a minute, and soon out of sight of what it waits for. Once it is $1.5$\,m
from its anchor, Nav2 brings it back. The stage's deadline still bounds
the wait, but the boat spends it where the task is, not drifting away from it.

\paragraph{Circling.} To circle an object at $\bm o$ with radius $R$ in $n$ steps,
start on the side facing the boat, $\beta = \operatorname{atan2}(p_y - o_y, p_x - o_x)$,
and place waypoints at angles $\beta + s\,2\pi k/n$ for $k = 1\ldots n$, with
$s=+1$ counter-clockwise and $-1$ clockwise. Each waypoint faces along the
circle, angle $+\,s\,\pi/2$, so Nav2 drives around the object rather than at it.

\paragraph{Relative routes.} A route given as (forward, left) offsets
$(f, l)$ from a pose $(x, y, \psi)$ maps to the world by the rotation
$\left(x + f\cos\psi - l\sin\psi,\ y + f\sin\psi + l\cos\psi\right)$.
\paragraph{Passing a gate.} A gate at $\bm m$ with heading $\phi$ is driven
through two poses, $\bm m - 1.5\,\bm f$ and $\bm m + 1.1\,\bm f$ with
$\bm f = (\cos\phi, \sin\phi)$: the second is half a hull ($0.53$\,m) and a margin
past the line, so the whole boat crosses it but the goal does not land on what
lies beyond. Whether a gate was cleared is judged by the boat's own pose, not by
whether Nav2 reached that last pose: with $\bm d = \bm p - \bm m$, the gate is
cleared once $\bm d\cdot\bm f \ge 0.6$ and $|\bm d\times\bm f| \le 2.5$.
The next gate is the nearest uncleared one ahead, ahead meaning along the last
cleared gate's heading, so a wobble of the boat's own heading never counts a
gate behind it.

\paragraph{Docking bays.} Banners stand along the dock's spine. With two or
more in view, the spine's direction is the principal axis of their positions,
$\theta = \tfrac12\operatorname{atan2}(2s_{xy}, s_{xx} - s_{yy})$ from their
covariances, and the bays open along its normal on the boat's side. A bay is
taken as empty when no vessel is tracked within $1$\,m of its centre. The boat
berths $1.2$\,m out from the banner: the bow clears the spine's costmap margin and
the stern stays inside a bay at least $2$\,m deep.

\paragraph{Counting.} Tracked positions closer than $1.5$\,m are one object, so a
track that is lost and found again is not counted twice.

\paragraph{What Nav2 takes literally.} Nav2 judges a route done by the distance to
its last pose, and drops waypoints within a metre of the boat. A full circle, or
a turn-in-place sweep back to the starting heading, ends where the boat already
is and would finish at once. So circles are sent as two halves and sweeps as two
single turns, each ending away from where it began.
