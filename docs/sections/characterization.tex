\section{Least squares and characterization}
\label{sec:char}

Speed curves, cross responses and magnetic interference are fitted by least
squares. These models are linear in their unknown coefficients, even when
their inputs include squares or sign-split efforts. Speed curves and signed
correction columns are described in Section~\ref{sec:thrust}.

\subsection{Least squares and the normal equations}

\paragraph{The unknowns, the data and the inputs.} For a measured cross response
$y$, the column fitter uses an affine function of the two cross-effort ratios
$c_1,c_2$. Stack the measured holds as rows:
\[
  \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}y_1\\\vdots\\y_n\end{bmatrix}}_{\bm y}
  \approx
  \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}1&c_{1,1}&c_{2,1}\\
  \vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\1&c_{1,n}&c_{2,n}\end{bmatrix}}_X
  \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\theta_0\\\theta_1\\\theta_2\end{bmatrix}}_{\bm\theta}.
\]
Each choice leaves a residual $\bm r=\bm y-X\bm\theta$, the part of the
measurement the model does not explain.

\paragraph{What is minimized.} We pick the $\bm\theta$ that makes the residual
smallest,
\[
  \hat{\bm\theta} = \argmin_{\bm\theta}\ J(\bm\theta),\qquad
  J(\bm\theta) = \|\bm y - X\bm\theta\|^2 = \sum_{k=1}^{n}(y_k-\theta_0-\theta_1c_{1,k}-\theta_2c_{2,k})^2 .
\]
Squaring makes misses of either sign count, punishes large misses more than
small ones, and makes $J$ a convex quadratic; independent design columns give a unique minimum.

\paragraph{Deriving the normal equations.} Expand $J$:
\[
  J(\bm\theta) = \bm y^\top\bm y - 2\,\bm\theta^\top X^\top\bm y + \bm\theta^\top X^\top X\,\bm\theta .
\]
The transpose appears because a squared length is a vector times itself,
$\|\bm r\|^2 = \bm r^\top\bm r$, and $(X\bm\theta)^\top = \bm\theta^\top X^\top$.
Using $\nabla_{\bm\theta}(\bm\theta^\top\bm c) = \bm c$ and
$\nabla_{\bm\theta}(\bm\theta^\top A\bm\theta) = 2A\bm\theta$ for symmetric $A$,
\[
  \nabla J = -2X^\top\bm y + 2X^\top X\,\bm\theta
           = -2X^\top(\bm y - X\bm\theta) = \bm 0
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  X^\top X\,\hat{\bm\theta} = X^\top \bm{y}.
\]
These are the \emph{normal equations}. The implementation uses
\texttt{numpy.linalg.lstsq} on $X$, with both cross responses as right-hand
sides, rather than solving $X^\top X$ directly.

\paragraph{What $X^\top$ means.} At the optimum, $X^\top\bm r = \bm 0$. Row $j$ of
$X^\top$ is column $j$ of $X$, so this says the residual has zero dot product
with every input column: with the column of ones (the misses average to zero),
with the $c_1$ column (no part of the miss still moves with $c_1$), and so on.
If any were nonzero, some input still explained part of the residual and the
corresponding coefficient could be improved. Geometrically, $X\hat{\bm\theta}$
is the orthogonal projection of $\bm y$ onto the span of the columns of $X$.
Multiplying by $X^\top$ also turns an $n$-row problem that has no exact solution
into a square $3\times3$ one that does. With $\bm c_k=(c_{1,k},c_{2,k})^\top$, the entries are plain sums,
\[
  X^\top X = \sum_k \begin{bmatrix}1\\ \bm c_k\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 & \bm c_k^\top\end{bmatrix},
  \qquad
  X^\top\bm y = \sum_k \begin{bmatrix}1\\ \bm c_k\end{bmatrix} y_k.
\]

\paragraph{Linear in the unknowns, not in the inputs.} Least squares needs the
model to be linear in $\bm\theta$, nothing more. A column of $X$ may be any known
function of the data: $\cos\psi_k$, $v_k|v_k|$, $|t_{i,k}|$. Part~I uses this
for the speed curves (Section~\ref{sec:thrust}), with columns $V$ and
$V|V|$, and the interference fit (Section~\ref{sec:corr-mag}), with efforts
split by sign.

\subsection{Two kinds of drift: the boat's and the weather's}

Mechanical bias is body-fixed; current and wind drift are expressed in the
world frame. The controller reads the configured mechanical bias and estimates
world drift online (Section~\ref{sec:controller}); the conditions estimator
measures drift from neutral windows.

\paragraph{The rotation.} Let $\dw = (d_x, d_y)$ be the drift velocity in the
world frame (ENU) and $\psi$ the heading, counter-clockwise from east. A body
vector is turned into the world by
\[
  R(\psi) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\psi & -\sin\psi\\ \sin\psi & \cos\psi \end{bmatrix},
\]
and a world vector into the body by its inverse, the transpose,
\[
  R(\psi)^\top \dw =
  \begin{bmatrix} \cos\psi & \sin\psi\\ -\sin\psi & \cos\psi \end{bmatrix}
  \begin{bmatrix} d_x\\ d_y \end{bmatrix}.
\]
\paragraph{Heading spread.} The physics analysis measures heading coverage by
the length of the mean unit heading vector,
\[
  \bar R=\left|\frac1n\sum_k(\cos\psi_k,\sin\psi_k)\right|.
\]
It is $1$ at a single heading and $0$ for headings spread evenly around the circle.

\paragraph{Limits.} The largest measured speed per axis gives the velocity
limits. Differentiating noisy odometry sample by sample inflates acceleration.
Velocity changes are taken over windows of at least
0.5\,s, from the full odometry including the switching transients, and the 99th
percentile of them is kept rather than the single largest.

\paragraph{Measured limits.} Limits describe the speed, acceleration and stopping
observed at the tested effort. A partial-throttle trial does not establish the
boat's full-throttle limits: those are not extrapolated by dividing speed by
$e_{\max}$ or acceleration by $e_{\max}^2$. Increase the test effort only in a
suitable area and review the measured limits before using them in Nav2. The
limits file contains only characterized keys and is merged at launch with the
current navigation profile; its reverse bound cannot exceed that profile's cap.
